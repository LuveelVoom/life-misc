Signals travel in straight lines at speed c through the lattice's rest frame, so the observer's picture at time T samples cell (x, y) at generation ⌊T − d/c⌋, where d is the straight-line distance from the observer's current position. This is classical finite-speed signalling (retardation and Doppler-like effects); there is no Lorentz contraction or time dilation, because Life has a preferred frame.

The 300 × 210 lattice wraps around at its edges so spaceships circulate forever, but light is only traced along direct paths inside the rectangle. The observer bounces off the edges.

What to look for: the two glider streams are compressed on the side the observer moves toward and stretched behind it; spaceships at four different speeds (c/2, 2c/7, c/7, c/10 in Life's native units) look faster when approaching and slower when receding; oscillators with periods 2, 3, 5, 8, 14, 15 and 30 fall out of sync with their near twins; the diehard keeps "living" for distant observers after it has vanished; eaters silently delete gliders. Above signal speed, light from behind is overtaken and those regions play backwards.
